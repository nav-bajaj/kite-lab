# Course glossary — approved first-use explanations

The canonical term bank for all course modules. Every market term used in
learner-facing content must be explained **idea-first** at its first use in
each document, and the explanation must agree with this file. The
`course-reviewer` subagent audits against it (see
`.claude/skills/course-voice/RUBRIC.md`).

Rules:

- One entry per term: the approved plain-language explanation, worded so it
  can be dropped into a sentence, plus the canonical analogy if one exists.
- Authors may vary the wording, not the meaning. If a better explanation is
  found while authoring, update the entry here in the same change.
- New terms found during review get proposed here by the reviewer and added
  when the module passes.
- Module column = where learners first meet the term properly.

## Core objects (Module 1)

| Term | Approved idea-first explanation | Canonical analogy |
|---|---|---|
| universe (research universe) | the list of stocks you've decided to ask your question about | election result means nothing until you know who was on the voter list |
| company | the actual business — factories, employees, brands, debts, cash flows — which exists whether or not anyone trades it | — |
| security | when you buy a share you own a claim on a slice of the business; the market's word for any tradable claim like this | — |
| share / ordinary equity share | a small slice of ownership in a business | — |
| exchange | the regulated marketplace where securities trade — the venue, not the merchandise | — |
| listing | a security being admitted to trade on a particular exchange; one company can be listed on several | — |
| index | a measuring stick: a basket of stocks picked by written rules, tracked as one number | picked like a cricket team — written rules, scheduled reviews, no lifetime guarantee |
| ETF | a fund you can buy and sell like a single stock | — |
| REIT | a security that gives you a slice of rental-property income | — |
| InvIT | a security that gives you a slice of infrastructure income (roads, power lines) | — |
| bond | a loan you make to a company or government that pays you interest and returns your money on a set date | — |

## Measurement terms (Module 1)

| Term | Approved idea-first explanation | Canonical analogy |
|---|---|---|
| market capitalization ("cap") | share price × number of shares — roughly what it would cost to buy the whole company | — |
| large / mid / small cap | the size ladder NSE Indices uses: biggest 100 companies are large cap, next 150 mid cap, next 250 small cap, beyond that microcap | ladder rungs |
| free float | the portion of a company's shares actually available for the public to buy and sell — promoters' locked-in holdings don't count | — |
| promoter | the founding or controlling owners of a company, whose large shareholdings are usually locked in and not available for the public to trade | — |
| free-float market-cap weighted | big companies' moves count for more: each stock influences the index in proportion to the market value of its publicly tradable shares | class average — toppers move it more |
| impact cost | how much the price moves against you just because you traded | — |
| breadth | how many stocks joined the move — a head-count where every stock gets one equal vote, big or small | how many students passed vs the class average |
| denominator / coverage | how many stocks were actually counted, out of the intended list, as of which date | — |
| as-of date | the date a changing fact was true; counts and lists go stale without one | photograph, not a portrait |
| reconstitution | the index's member list gets refreshed on a schedule (Nifty 500: twice a year, end of March and September) | team selection day |
| constituent | a member stock of the index | squad member |
| survivorship bias | judging the past using today's list studies only the companies that survived and succeeded; the failed and delisted ones have vanished from the record | judging a journey by interviewing only the finishers |
| 200-DMA (200-day moving average) | a stock's average closing price over its last 200 trading days — a slow-moving line that shows its long-term trend | — |
| slippage | the gap between the price you expected and the price you actually got, because your own trade moved it; the index's eligibility measure of this is the impact cost | — |
| demat account | the account that stores the shares you've bought, the way a bank account stores your money | — |
| portfolio | the specific stocks you actually own right now — different from a universe, which is the list you've decided to measure whether you own it or not | — |
