From SIP to stock sense.

A practical beginner course for young Indian investors who started with mutual funds and now want to understand markets, investigate stocks, and build a disciplined process. The promise is competence, not returns.

4 weeks 8 modules 30–45 min each 7 core artifacts 1 local AI lab

The learner

Curious enough to begin. Missing a way to proceed.

Age 22–28. Has a mutual-fund SIP, recognizes some stock-market terms, uses AI chat and search, and is willing to learn—but does not yet have a repeatable way to judge an idea.

Starts here
“I follow the market, but I am not sure what the numbers mean or how to judge an idea.”
Graduates here
“I can explain the market, test a simple rule, spot weak evidence, and know what I still need to learn.”

The emotional outcome: calmer, more skeptical, and more capable—not falsely certain.

The graduate profile

What can the learner actually do at the end?

Completion means explaining, comparing, testing, and reflecting—not simply reaching the end of a playlist. Every goal leaves evidence.

01Market-plumbing map

Explain how the Indian market works

Trace how a company raises capital and how an order becomes a settled security in a demat account. Explain exchanges, brokers, depositories, clearing, indices, and corporate actions in plain language.

02Product comparison grid

Ask better questions about products

Compare stocks, mutual funds, ETFs, debt, REITs, InvITs, gold, IPOs, and derivatives by ownership, return source, risk, liquidity, cost, tax questions, and effort—without reducing the answer to a ranking.

03Market weather report

Read the Indian market today

Use direction, breadth, leadership, concentration, volatility, and context to write a dated market view. Separate what the data shows from what the learner infers.

04Observation dossier

Investigate a stock without chasing a tip

Frame a business question, note price and risk evidence, identify missing fundamentals, and record what would disprove a thesis. Recognize when deeper research or a registered professional is needed.

05Portfolio policy

Design a portfolio process

Explain diversification, drawdown, position sizing, rebalancing, and benchmarking. Compare portfolio philosophies by their trade-offs, then write rules that make decisions consistent.

06AI strategy pack

Use AI as a junior analyst—not an oracle

Turn an idea into an inspectable rule, build a paper backtest locally, challenge its assumptions, and explain why it could be wrong. Recognize data, AI, fraud, and behavioral red flags before acting.

The course engine

One loop, repeated until it becomes a habit.

Marketworks is the live practice surface. Every module asks the learner to use real observations and leave a trace of their reasoning.

AI Portfolio Lab

Build a paper strategy. Then try to break it.

Codex or Claude Code acts as a local implementation partner. No prior coding is assumed: guided prompts begin with the simple codex or claude command. The learner owns the rules and reviews every file.

The lab succeeds when the learner can explain why the backtest may be wrong—not when the chart goes up.

Paper only

Synthetic or appropriately licensed delayed end-of-day data. No real money or orders.

No credentials

No broker connection, API keys, demat details, account data, or personal amounts.

One simple rule

No parameter hunt, leverage, options, or return optimization.

Human accountable

AI use is disclosed. The learner reviews assumptions, limitations, and every final claim.

Trust boundary

Conditions, not instructions.

The course is designed to increase learner agency without disguising education as personalized advice.

Educational scope

No personalized advice, named buy/sell calls, price targets, guarantees, or trade execution.

Dated evidence

Market views carry an as-of date and distinguish observation, inference, and uncertainty.

Safe AI use

No credentials or personal portfolio data; paper exercises only; assumptions and AI assistance disclosed.

Compliance review

Public strategy templates, model comparisons, security analysis, and performance claims require review before launch.
