Marketworks Foundations

From SIP to Stock Sense.

A practical beginner course for young Indian investors who have started with mutual funds and now want to understand markets, investigate stocks, and build a disciplined process. The promise is competence, not returns.

  • 4 weeks
  • 8 modules
  • 30–45 min each
  • 7 core artifacts
  • 1 local AI lab
The learner

Age 22–28. Has a mutual-fund SIP, recognizes some stock-market terms, uses AI chat and search, and is curious—but does not yet have a repeatable way to learn or decide.

Starts here

“I follow the market, but I am not sure what the numbers mean or how to judge an idea.”

Graduates here

“I can explain the market, test a simple rule, spot weak evidence, and know what I still need to learn.”

Emotional outcome: calmer, more skeptical, and more capable—not falsely certain.

Why this course, now

SEBI’s 2025 investor survey describes an “aware beginner” audience: familiarity is growing faster than understanding.

63% of surveyed households knew at least one securities-market product.
9.5% participated in the securities market—the confidence gap is still large.
64% of investors had limited market knowledge, despite already participating.

The graduate profile

What can the learner actually do at the end?

Each goal produces visible evidence. Completion means explaining, comparing, testing, and reflecting—not simply watching all the videos.

01

Explain how the market works

Trace how an Indian company raises capital and how an order becomes a settled security in a demat account. Explain exchanges, brokers, depositories, clearing, indices, and corporate actions in plain language.

Evidence: a market-plumbing map
02

Choose better questions about products

Compare stocks, mutual funds, ETFs, debt, REITs, InvITs, gold, IPOs, and derivatives by ownership, return source, risk, liquidity, cost, tax questions, and effort—without reducing the answer to a ranking.

Evidence: a product comparison grid
03

Read the Indian market today

Use direction, breadth, leadership, concentration, volatility, and context to write a dated market view. Separate what the data shows from what the learner infers.

Evidence: a five-line market weather report
04

Investigate a stock without chasing a tip

Frame a business question, note price and risk evidence, identify missing fundamentals, and record the conditions that would disprove a thesis. Recognize when deeper research or a registered professional is needed.

Evidence: an observation dossier
05

Design a portfolio process

Explain diversification, drawdown, position sizing, rebalancing, and benchmarking. Compare portfolio philosophies by their trade-offs, then write rules that make decisions consistent.

Evidence: a one-page portfolio policy
06

Use AI as a junior analyst—not an oracle

Turn an idea into an inspectable rule, use Codex or Claude Code to build a paper backtest locally, challenge its assumptions, and explain why the result could be wrong. Recognize data, AI, fraud, and behavioral red flags before acting.

Evidence: strategy card, report, tests, and failure memo

The meaningful change

Not more market vocabulary. Better market judgment.

Market news
Sees a Nifty headline and a few winners.
Checks direction, participation, leadership, risk, and the data date.
Stock ideas
Asks, “Is this a good stock?”
Asks what evidence supports the idea, what is missing, and what would falsify it.
Portfolio
Collects holdings without a shared logic.
Writes rules for purpose, universe, size, review, rebalancing, and risk.
AI
Asks a chatbot what to buy.
Gives an agent a frozen specification, reviews its work, and red-teams the result.
Confidence
Mistakes certainty and recent returns for skill.
Can act with a process while naming uncertainty, limitations, and boundaries.

The course engine

One loop, repeated until it becomes a habit.

Marketworks is the live practice surface. Every module asks the learner to do something with real observations and leave a trace of their reasoning.

01

Observe

Open a relevant Marketworks view and notice the current evidence.

02

Explain

Translate the reading into plain English before forming a conclusion.

03

Test

Apply a checklist, comparison, or simple rule to challenge the first impression.

04

Reflect

Record what remains uncertain, what would change the view, and when to return.

AI Portfolio Lab

Build a paper strategy. Then try to break it.

Codex or Claude Code acts as a local implementation partner. No prior coding is assumed: guided prompts begin with the simple codex or claude command. The learner owns the idea, freezes the rules before seeing results, reviews every file, and keeps all work on their own device.

The lab succeeds when the learner can explain why the backtest may be wrong—not when the chart goes up.

Paper only

Synthetic or appropriately licensed delayed end-of-day data. No real money or orders.

No credentials

No broker connection, API keys, demat details, account data, or personal portfolio amounts.

One simple rule

One signal choice and one risk rule. No parameter hunt, leverage, options, or return optimization.

Human accountable

AI use is disclosed. The learner reviews assumptions, output, limitations, and every final claim.

Trust boundary

Conditions, not instructions.

Educational scope

No personalized advice, named buy/sell calls, price targets, guarantees, or trade execution.

Dated evidence

Market views carry an as-of date and distinguish observation, inference, and uncertainty.

Safe AI use

No credentials or personal portfolio data; paper exercises only; assumptions and AI assistance disclosed.

Compliance review

Public strategy templates, model comparisons, security analysis, and performance claims require legal review before launch.
