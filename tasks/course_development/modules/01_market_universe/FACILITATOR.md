# Module 1 facilitator guide — What exactly is "the market"?

## Teaching objective

Move the learner from:

> "The market means whatever index or stock list happens to be on my screen."

to:

> "Before I believe a market claim, I ask: which list of stocks, as of which
> date, weighted or one-vote-each, how many were actually counted, and what
> was left out?"

## 60-minute run-of-show

| Time | Segment | Learner action | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-5 | Hook: how many stocks? | Give and revise an initial answer | Recognizes ambiguity |
| 5-13 | Five objects | Sort company, security, exchange, index, universe | Part A |
| 13-22 | Indian-market funnel | Complete listing-to-universe map | Part B |
| 22-32 | Nifty 500 construction | Explain eligibility, size ladder, weighting, review | Quiz checks 3-6 |
| 32-42 | Marketworks walkthrough | Locate universe, date, weighting, denominator | Universe contract |
| 42-50 | Weighted index vs breadth | Calculate ten-stock fixture | Part D |
| 50-56 | Universe decision card | State fit and blind spots | Module artifact |
| 56-60 | Quiz + exit ticket | Complete core check | 6/8, Q7 required |

The walkthrough can run 12-15 minutes in a live cohort. If it reaches 15,
shorten whole-group discussion in the decision-card segment, not the
index-versus-breadth exercise.

# Opening script

Display:

> "The Indian market rose today."

Ask:

1. "Which market are we talking about?"
2. "Which stocks were measured — and whose list is that?"
3. "Did every stock count equally, or did the big ones count for more?"
4. "As of when?"
5. "How many stocks actually joined the rise?"

Then ask:

> "How many stocks are in the Indian stock market? Take a guess — say a
> number out loud."

Accept 50, 500, 750, 2,000+, and "I don't know." Write the answers side by
side.

Say:

> "Here's the fun part: this isn't a trivia question with one secret answer.
> Almost every number you just said is correct — about a different thing.
> Fifty is the headline number you see every evening — that kind of
> rules-based basket, tracked as one figure, is called an index. Thousands
> is everything listed on the exchanges — the regulated marketplaces, like
> NSE and BSE, where shares are bought and sold. None of them, by itself,
> is 'the market.' Today's job is
> learning to name the thing before we read it."

## Slide 6 — Nifty 500 eligibility

## Part B

Answers for the blanks, in the order they appear in the worksheet funnel:

```text
1. exchanges     EVERYTHING LISTED ON INDIAN EXCHANGES
2. NSE           COMPANIES LISTED ON NSE
3. small         a separate platform for very small companies
4. shares        ORDINARY SHARES THAT ACTUALLY TRADE
5. 500           NIFTY 500
6. 100 / 150 / 250   large / mid-sized / smaller companies
7. date          the list as it stood on a specific date
8. measure       how many stocks we could actually measure
```

Accept close synonyms only when the learner can explain the idea behind the
word (e.g. "count" for "measure" is fine if they can say what gets counted
and why some stocks can't be).

## Part C

### Question 1

- Preferred: Nifty 50.
- Limitation: focuses on the largest highly liquid companies and does not
  describe wider participation.

### Question 2

- Preferred: Nifty 500.
- Limitation: excludes the microcap/broader tail and still has changing
  membership.

### Question 3

- Preferred: Nifty Total Market or a suitable custom microcap universe.
- Limitation: wider tail adds liquidity, history, and data-quality challenges.

### Question 4

- Preferred: the dated Marketworks Nifty 500-derived universe, not an abstract
  timeless list.
- Required metadata: membership date, price-data date, intended count,
  eligible denominator, and missing count.

## Part D

Approximate weighted return:

```text
A: +0.60%
B: +0.20%
C-J combined: -0.50%
Index: +0.30%
```

- advancing: 2;
- declining: 8;
- percentage advancing: 20%;
- completion: index positive, participation weak/narrow.

One does not invalidate the other.

## Part E — strong example

```text
QUESTION
How broad is the trend across a practical large-, mid-, and small-cap Indian
equity universe?

OFFICIAL REFERENCE
Nifty 500.

PRODUCT UNIVERSE
The Marketworks member-list snapshot named in the course manifest, with its
membership date and data-coverage report.

ELIGIBILITY
An ordinary share of an Indian company on NSE that trades regularly, has
enough publicly tradable shares (free float), is cheap to trade in and out of
(low impact cost), and ranks high enough on trading activity and company
value.

SIZE COVERAGE
100 large, 150 mid, 250 small.

WEIGHTING
Official index: bigger companies count for more, in proportion to their
publicly tradable value (free-float market-cap weighted).

COUNTING
Breadth: one equal vote for each stock actually measured that day.

DATE AND COVERAGE
Membership date: the snapshot date named in the course manifest.
Intended members: the official Nifty 500 count for that date.
Stocks we could actually measure: the count with enough usable history
that day (fewer when a metric needs history a new member lacks).
Missing or left out: recently joined stocks without 200 days of history,
noted explicitly rather than hidden.

WHY THIS UNIVERSE
Covers most of the market's value, spans large, mid, and small companies, is
already screened for tradability, and gives everyone the same shared list to
measure.

BLIND SPOTS
The smallest listed companies sit outside the list entirely. Being a member
says nothing about how risky or how fairly priced a stock is. The list
changes twice a year — and judging the past with today's list quietly drops
the companies that failed or left along the way.

PLAIN-LANGUAGE EXPLANATION
Marketworks starts with the Nifty 500 because it is a wide, rules-based,
dated list of India's large, mid, and small companies — enough stocks to
compare, already screened so they trade cleanly, and shared so everyone
measures the same thing. It is a learning laboratory, not a list of good
stocks.
```

# Misconception responses

## "So the Nifty 500 is the safest 500 stocks?"

Response:

> "That's a reasonable thought — the entry filters do sound like a safety
> check. Here's the missing piece: the filters only ask whether a stock
> trades enough to be measured cleanly. They never ask whether the business
> is good, whether the price is fair, or whether the stock suits you. A
> small company in the index can still be a wild, risky ride. Filtered is
> not the same as recommended."

## "Why not just use the Nifty 50? It drives the headlines."

Response:

> "The Nifty 50 is a fine tool — when your question is about the giants. But
> some questions it simply can't answer. If you want to know whether
> mid-sized and smaller companies are rising too, or sitting the rally out,
> fifty giants can't tell you that. Pick the list to fit the question, not
> the question to fit the list."

## "Why not use every listed company and be fully comprehensive?"

Response:

> "'Everything' sounds thorough, and one day it can be a real research
> project. But the far end of the market is a different animal as data:
> stocks that barely trade, short histories, gaps and errors. Pouring all of
> that into your first laboratory doesn't make the answer more complete — it
> makes it harder to trust. Start with a clean room. Add the mess later, on
> purpose, with rules for handling it."

## "If Marketworks has 500 symbols, why does a metric sometimes show fewer?"

Response:

> "Good catch — being on the list and being measurable are two different
> things. Take a metric like '% above the 200-day average' — that average
> is each stock's average closing price over its last 200 trading days, a
> slow line showing its long-term trend. Computing it needs 200 days of
> price history, and a stock that joined the market recently doesn't have
> that yet. So on some days we measure, say, 480 of the 500 — and the screen
> should tell you exactly that. The count of stocks actually measured is
> what we call the denominator."

## "Why does Marketworks say NSE 500 if the official name is Nifty 500?"

Response:

> "'NSE 500' is our product's nickname for our dated copy of the member
> list, which is built from NSE-listed stocks. When we're being precise, we
> say 'Nifty 500' for the official index and 'Marketworks Nifty 500-derived
> universe' for our dated snapshot. Two names, two different objects — and
> keeping them apart also stops you from mixing up the exchange (NSE) with
> the index (Nifty 500)."

# Facilitation guardrails

Do:

- say "starting learning/research universe";
- date all changing counts and coverage statistics;
- explain that broader is not automatically better;
- distinguish membership from usable-data coverage;
- explain official index weighting versus breadth counting; and
- disclose current-snapshot limitations.

Do not:

- say learners should put their first direct-stock money into these companies;
- imply every constituent is safe, liquid, or high quality;
- call the Nifty 500 the entire Indian market;
- present index inclusion as due diligence;
- add BSE and NSE company counts; or
- discuss an individual constituent as a course recommendation.

# Instructor-only product note

The current repository's `data/static/nse500_universe.csv` contained 500 unique
`EQ` symbols across 20 industry labels when inspected on 28 July 2026. The
production breadth code describes this as a current-snapshot universe and
explicitly warns that delisted former members are absent from long history.

This internal check is not a permanent published fact. Before each cohort:

1. read the course universe manifest;
2. record its effective/membership date;
3. compare it with the current official constituent file;
4. record missing histories;
5. display the metric-eligible denominator; and
6. decide whether any mismatch requires a course-data refresh.

The index is designed around 500 companies, but a dated official factsheet or
constituent file can publish a different security count around maintenance or
corporate actions. Preserve the official count and date, check official
maintenance notices, and never invent a reason for the difference.

# Exit-ticket standard

Pass if the learner's answer includes at least:

- named universe;
- as-of or membership date; and
- weighting/counting or denominator.

Strong answer:

> "Which dated universe are you calling the market, how are its members
> selected and counted, and how many have usable data?"
