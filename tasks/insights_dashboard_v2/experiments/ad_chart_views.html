Advances & declines — presentation experiments

Six ways to draw the same two series: the NSE 500 cumulative A-D line (in stocks, through 2026-08-19) and the Nifty 500 index. Real data from the local breadth engine. Toggle range and theme; hover any chart for the day's values.

What TradingView does (from today's session)

  • Breadth never shares a canvas with price. Their built-in Advance Decline Line renders in its own pane below the price pane — own scale, shared time axis, one crosshair through both. The two shapes align vertically instead of overlapping.
  • Each pane carries its own compact legend (name + live value, top-left) and its own last-value chip on the axis, so no number is ever ambiguous.
  • Daily flavours are drawn as columns, not lines — their Advance/Decline Ratio (Bars) is a histogram, which matches how you read a day-at-a-time series.
  • When they do overlay a second symbol on one pane, it gets a visible % axis on the left — an overlay you cannot read a level off is treated as a defect.
A

Baseline — what production ships today

A-D line as a filled area, index overlay as a thin muted line on a hidden scale. Reference point for everything below.

Known weakness: the gray overlay disappears against the grid, and its level can't be read anywhere except the crosshair row.

B

Two panes — the TradingView pattern

Index on top, A-D line below, shared time axis and crosshair. No hidden scale, no rebasing caveat: both series keep their real units and you read correlation by vertical alignment. This is how every charting platform ships breadth.

Assessment: divergences (index up while A-D flattens) are much easier to spot than in A; costs ~80px of height. lightweight-charts v5 supports panes natively, so this fits the existing component.

C

Two panes — daily net advances as columns

Same layout, but the lower pane draws the day-at-a-time series the tab already reports as "Net advances (daily)": advancers minus decliners as a share of movers, coloured by sign. TradingView draws exactly this as its Advance/Decline Ratio (Bars).

Assessment: the histogram reads as breadth pressure — clusters of red under a rising index jump out. Could replace the cumulative line for the "today" story, or sit beside it as a second view.

D

Overlay kept, made legible

Minimal change to production: same single canvas, but the index line moves from muted gray to the amber counter-series token, gets 1.5px of weight, and both series get colour-chipped names in the readout row.

Assessment: the overlay stops vanishing and the two shapes stay comparable. Still a hidden scale — the rebasing footnote stays. Cheapest real improvement if panes feel like too much change.

E

Overlay with a visible % axis on the left

The TradingView compare treatment: index rebased to % change from the left edge of the window, but on a visible left axis, so its level is readable anywhere. A-D keeps the right axis.

drag to pan · wheel / pinch to zoom · drag an axis to stretch it · double-click an axis to reset it · the % rebase follows the left edge as you move

Assessment: the overlay finally has a readable level, and the 0% line doubles as "flat since window start". The cost: it is openly a dual-axis chart — the shapes' vertical alignment is still arbitrary, which is the argument for B instead.

F

A-D as a baseline chart — direction since window start

The A-D line's level is meaningless; only its direction matters. A baseline series anchors the fill at the window's first value: green above, red below, so "is breadth net-up over what I'm looking at" is answered by colour before you read anything.

drag to pan · wheel / pinch to zoom · double-click an axis to reset it · the green/red anchor re-bases to the first visible day as you move

Assessment: strongest at answering the tab's core question at a glance, and it re-anchors itself every time the range changes. Pairs naturally with the pane layout in B (index above, this below).
