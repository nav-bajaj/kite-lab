One engine, two markets.

We took the exact momentum configurations that run our Indian production portfolios — Core Momentum and Quality Momentum — and ran them on U.S. large-caps. Not a single parameter retuned. Seven full years, 2019 through 2025. This note shows what came back.

49.1% a year for seven years, against the NIFTY 100's 13.5%. Still ₹93L after a flat 25% tax on each year's gains — the same engine that runs in your dashboard today.

Core Momentum · United States $1M → $9.9M

38.7% a year on U.S. large-caps — with zero retuning — against the S&P 500's 17.2%. $6.1M after the same 25% annual tax. The signal is not an Indian quirk.

The best risk-adjusted run in the study — 48.2% a year with the shallowest drawdown of any strategy or index here (−32.9%).

What we take from this

Momentum is a property of markets, not of one market.

The premium travels.

Both strategies beat their home index by 17–36 percentage points a year, in both countries, under parameters that had never seen a U.S. price. That is the strongest kind of out-of-sample evidence we can produce.

India is the stronger home.

Quality Momentum's Sharpe falls from 2.26 in India to 1.23 in the U.S. — its quality signal was tuned on emerging-market dispersion, and U.S. large-caps are a tighter, more crowded field.

Quality Momentum changes character abroad.

At home it is the defensive book — the shallowest drawdown in the study. On U.S. data it carried the deepest (−41.2%, mostly the 2022 Fed cycle). Same rules, different market structure.

The two markets complement each other.

India carried 2021–22 while the U.S. went sideways; the U.S. carried 2025 (+60.6% for Core Momentum) while Indian momentum rested at −8.6%. Different years, different leaders — the discipline is the constant.

We didn't fit a strategy to a market. We found a behaviour that repeats — and wrote it down as rules.

How to read these numbers

The honest fine print — the same caveats we hold ourselves to internally.

Backtested, not live

These are simulated results from historical prices, not client returns. Indian curves use the exact production configurations; U.S. curves come from our May 2026 cross-market study. Both start trading in 2017 so the 2019 window opens with a realistic, already-invested portfolio.

Costs are counted

Every trade is charged 20 basis points of slippage per side in both markets. For U.S. large-caps that is conservative — real spreads are tighter — so the U.S. figures are, if anything, understated.

Universe construction

India trades the NSE 500 (Core) and Nifty 250 (Quality); the U.S. runs use S&P 500 ∪ Nasdaq 100 (516 names). Both use today's index membership, which flatters absolute returns somewhat — but identically on both sides, so the India–U.S. comparison itself is clean.

What "unchanged" means

Lookbacks, ranking rules, position counts, rebalance cadence, exits, stops — all locked at Indian production values. The only substitutions are the price panel, the trading calendar, and the regime index (NIFTY 100 → SPY).

How the tax view is modelled

The after-tax toggle deducts a flat 25% of each calendar year's net gain at year-end, with losses carried forward to offset future gains. Benchmarks stay untaxed — a buy-and-hold investor defers tax until sale. The flat rate is a deliberate simplification, not a reflection of actual short- and long-term capital-gains rates in either country.

Hypothetical performance has inherent limitations: it is constructed with hindsight, does not reflect actual capital at risk, and past performance — simulated or real — does not guarantee future results. Tax figures use a simplified flat rate and are not tax advice. Nothing here is investment advice or an offer of any product.
