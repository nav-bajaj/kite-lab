# Momentum Investing: Five-Reel Series

## Series brief

- **Audience:** Indian investors who are new to stock picking
- **Purpose:** Explain momentum investing and introduce quantitative, rules-based portfolios
- **Language:** Simple, spoken and free of unnecessary jargon
- **Length:** Approximately 75–85 seconds per reel, with 90 seconds as the hard ceiling
- **Content boundary:** Conceptual education, without stock recommendations or performance claims
- **Persistent footer:** `Educational content. Not investment advice.`

## Visual language

All reels are produced in a `9:16` mobile frame using three layouts:

1. **Full-screen talking head** for hooks, caveats and closing thoughts.
2. **Stacked split** with the speaker above and the graphics canvas below.
3. **Full-screen graphics canvas** for charts, comparisons and abstract visual explanations.

The stacked split uses this default composition:

```text
┌────────────────────┐
│                    │
│   TALKING HEAD     │  44%
│                    │
├────────────────────┤
│                    │
│  GRAPHICS CANVAS   │  56%
│                    │
└────────────────────┘
```

Keep the speaker above by default. Captions sit near the bottom of the speaker region so they do not obstruct the graphics. A speaker-below layout is reserved for the rare scene in which the graphic needs the upper portion of the frame.

### Design system

- Background: `#F0F7F3`
- Ink: `#1A1A1A`
- Primary green: `#0C7A62`
- Signal green: `#55C374`
- Marigold accent: `#E8A33D`
- Neutral chart line: `#8C9490`
- Outfit for captions, labels and body copy
- Fraunces for short cover titles only
- White cards with soft shadows, rounded corners and a faint quant-grid texture

Charts are conceptual and carry a small `Illustration only` label. They do not show fabricated returns, axes or company data. Avoid rockets, flames, coins, fake trading terminals and decorative candlestick charts.

## Reel 1: What momentum actually means

**Cover:** Why would anyone invest after a stock has risen?

**Target runtime:** Approximately 81 seconds

### Spoken script

Momentum investing starts with an idea that feels wrong the first time you hear it: a rising stock isn't automatically expensive, and a falling stock isn't automatically a bargain.

Momentum is the tendency of stocks that have been doing better than their peers to keep doing better for a while. The key word is *peers*. If one stock has risen, but the rest of its market has risen much more, it may still have weak momentum.

A rules-based momentum portfolio turns that observation into a repeatable process. It starts with a broad group of liquid Indian stocks, measures their recent price strength, ranks them, and holds a basket of the stronger names.

That is very different from chasing a stock because it jumped this morning or because it's trending on social media. Momentum looks across a longer period and compares every stock using the same rule.

So the core idea is simple: price strength can contain information. Momentum investing measures that strength instead of guessing where the next hot stock will come from.

## Reel 2: Why can momentum persist?

**Cover:** Why can a rising stock keep rising?

**Target runtime:** Approximately 79 seconds

### Spoken script

Why would a stock that has already gone up keep going up? Because markets can be fast, but people still take time to change their minds.

Imagine a listed Indian company starts reporting better business results. Some investors notice immediately. Others wait for another quarter. Large institutions may take time to research the change and build a position without moving the price too sharply.

That gradual recognition matters. Investors often anchor to the old story, so they may underreact when the facts begin to change. Later, as the improvement becomes harder to ignore, more buyers may arrive. The price trend continues because the information is being absorbed in stages.

This is one explanation for momentum. It isn't the only one, and it doesn't mean a rising price must keep rising. Markets reverse, and expectations can run too far.

Momentum only needs a smaller claim to be useful: sometimes, a real change takes time to travel through the market. The strategy tries to follow that adjustment while it is happening, then step away when the evidence weakens.

## Reel 3: How a quant portfolio works

**Cover:** What does a quant know that you don't?

**Estimated runtime:** Approximately 75–80 seconds

### Spoken script

What does a quantitative portfolio know about a company that you don't? Often, nothing. Its advantage is that it follows the same process every time.

*Quantitative* simply means the decisions are written as measurable rules. For a momentum portfolio, the process might begin with a defined universe, such as a broad group of liquid NSE-listed stocks.

Each stock receives a score based on its recent price strength. It is then ranked against the other eligible stocks, like a league table. The portfolio is built from a basket near the top, with position limits so one company cannot dominate the result.

On a fixed schedule, the table is calculated again. Stocks that still meet the rules remain. Those that no longer qualify make room for stronger names.

The computer isn't predicting a target price or deciding which founder tells the best story. It keeps asking one consistent question: which stocks are leading now?

That consistency is the point. A quant portfolio turns an investment idea into a process that can be repeated and tested.

## Reel 4: Momentum is also a selling system

**Cover:** Finding winners is only half the strategy

**Estimated runtime:** Approximately 80–85 seconds

### Spoken script

Most people think momentum investing is about finding winners. The harder part is admitting when yesterday's winner no longer leads.

That is why rebalancing matters. *Rebalancing* means reviewing the portfolio on a fixed schedule and adjusting it back to its rules.

On each review date, every eligible stock is ranked again. A holding can stay even after a small fall if its rank remains strong. But when its strength fades beyond a defined limit, the portfolio removes it and makes room for a stronger name.

Without that rule, it is easy to change the story halfway through. A stock entered because of momentum can become a long-term conviction simply because selling feels uncomfortable. The rule forces a cleaner question: does the original evidence still exist?

This discipline is not painless. In a sideways market, stocks can move in and out of the rankings, creating false starts and higher trading costs.

The purpose of rebalancing isn't to avoid every mistake. It is to stop one old decision from becoming permanent. Momentum doesn't demand being right about a company forever. It demands updating when the evidence changes.

## Reel 5: Rules don't remove risk

**Cover:** Does rules-based investing make a portfolio safe?

**Estimated runtime:** Approximately 80–85 seconds

### Spoken script

If a portfolio is run by rules, does that make it safe? No. It makes the risk easier to see.

Momentum can struggle when the market changes direction suddenly. After a sharp fall, beaten-down stocks may rebound quickly while the previous leaders stall. A portfolio designed to follow strength can then find itself on the wrong side of the reversal.

Portfolio rules can shape this risk. A basket spreads exposure across companies. Position limits keep one stock from becoming too large. Liquidity rules avoid names that may be difficult to trade. Some systems also use a *market regime rule*, a simple check on whether the broad market is trending up or down, before setting exposure.

None of this removes uncertainty. Rebalancing creates costs and tax consequences. Rules can lag a fast market. A strategy can underperform for an uncomfortable stretch, and a beautiful backtest can still disappoint in real life.

The case for quantitative momentum rests on transparency, not certainty. You can know what enters, what exits, and which evidence changes the portfolio. Its real promise is a repeatable decision process, with its weaknesses visible from the start.

## Production workflow

- **Premiere Pro:** Talking-head edit, colour correction, sound mix, captions and final assembly.
- **After Effects:** Stacked-split template, masks, card shadows and subtle parallax for AI-generated stills.
- **Remotion:** Conceptual charts, ranking cards, information ripple, repeatable typography and full-screen graphics canvases.
- **Captions:** Outfit SemiBold, two lines maximum, positioned near the bottom of the talking-head region during stacked scenes and above platform controls during full-screen scenes.
- **Sound:** A restrained music bed with soft card ticks and line-draw textures. Avoid a whoosh on every transition.

Graphics can be exported from Remotion as full-frame ProRes files. Overlays intended to sit above the speaker can be exported with an alpha channel for assembly in Premiere Pro.
